Possessive forms with the noun ending

In the pattern from your notes, the noun takes a possessive ending that commonly appears as -ս for “my” and -դ for “your” (with spelling/pronunciation changes in some forms). Learn the noun and its ending together.

ArmenianEnglish
գիրքսmy book
մեքենասmy car
գիրքդyour book
մեքենադyour car
Important distinction: իմ գիրքը (“my book”) uses a possessive word before the noun, while գիրքս (“my book”) attaches the possessive ending -ս directly to the noun. Likewise, գիրքդ means “your book” (singular informal). The noun’s definite ending may change when a possessive ending is used.

Unit 03 · Present

The present tense: “to be”

Use the present forms of լինել (“to be”) to say who someone is or how something is.

Forms of “to be”

PronounArmenianEnglish
Եսեմam
Դուեսare
Նաէis
Մենքենքare
Դուքեքare
Նրանքենare

Word order

Armenian sentence structure often places the main verb towards the end. The exact order can vary with emphasis and sentence type.

Ես ուսանող եմ։
I am a student.
Նա այստեղ է։
He / she is here.
Learn each form together with its pronoun rather than memorising the endings alone. The verb լինել (to be) is irregular and must be memorised as a set of forms: եմ, ես, է, ենք, եք, են.

Irregular verbs in the present tense

Some common verbs do not follow one simple regular pattern. Memorise the present forms as a set and practise them in sentences.

For these verbs, the common Eastern Armenian present form is the -ում form plus the present form of լինել (to be). The auxiliary changes with the subject.

Unit 04 · Past

Past tense I and II

The past tense is used for actions that happened and were completed in the past.

How to approach past-tense verbs

Your notes distinguish two past forms. The first uses the past form of լինել (“to be”), for example ես էի — “I was” and ես չէի — “I was not”. Past Tense II (the aorist) describes a completed past action, for example երգեցի — “I sang”.

երգել → երգեցի — to sing → I sang
խաղալ → խաղացի — to play → I played
Ես էի — I was
Ես չէի — I was not

For many verbs, the aorist uses a past stem plus person endings; some frequent verbs are irregular and must be memorised separately.

Irregular verbs: do not assume every verb follows the regular -ել or -ալ pattern. Learn irregular past stems and forms individually from the verb tables in your notes; practise each irregular verb in the past-tense table below as part of this tense lesson, not as a separate verb category.
